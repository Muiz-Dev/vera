# Overview

The Team Engine provides structured collaboration within an Organization.

While an Organization represents the entire company or workspace, a Team represents a smaller operational unit with its own members, responsibilities, permissions, and resources.

Examples include:

* Backend Team
* Frontend Team
* Mobile Team
* DevOps Team
* Security Team
* Product Team
* QA Team
* Customer Success Team

A single Organization may contain hundreds or thousands of Teams.

The Team Engine enables organizations to scale collaboration without sacrificing security or operational clarity.

---

The Team Engine provides:

# Vision

The Team Engine should enable organizations of every size to organize developers into logical working groups while maintaining clear ownership, efficient collaboration, and secure resource isolation.

# Core Principles

## Teams Belong to Organizations

A Team cannot exist without an Organization.

```text id="team01"
Organization

↓

Teams

↓

Members
```

---

## Members Can Join Multiple Teams

A developer may belong to several Teams.

Example

```text id="team02"
Developer

↓

Backend Team

↓

DevOps Team

↓

Security Team
```

---

## Teams Own Resources

Resources may belong directly to a Team.

Examples

* Applications
* Secrets
* API Keys
* Storage
* Webhooks
* Pipelines

---

## Least Privilege

Teams should only access resources assigned to them.

---

## Independent Lifecycle

Teams can be created, archived, restored, or deleted independently of their Organization.

# Responsibilities

The Team Engine is responsible for

* Team creation
* Team updates
* Team deletion
* Team membership
* Team invitations
* Team metadata
* Team settings
* Team ownership
* Team activity

The Team Engine is NOT responsible for

* Authentication
* Billing
* Authorization policy evaluation
* Deployment execution

# Architecture Decision Record (ADR-004)

**Decision:** Teams are organizational collaboration units that exist beneath Organizations but above individual Developers.

**Reason:** Separating Teams from Organizations provides a scalable collaboration model where permissions, resources, and responsibilities can be delegated without increasing administrative complexity.

**Consequences:**

* Teams inherit organizational context.
* Developers can belong to multiple Teams.
* Resources may be scoped to Teams instead of entire Organizations.
* Large enterprises can organize work into secure, manageable units.

---

# Design Goals

The Team Engine provides structured collaboration within Vera by enabling Organizations to organize developers into secure, resource-aware working groups.

It establishes a scalable collaboration model that supports growing engineering organizations while maintaining strong access control, clear ownership, operational flexibility, and seamless integration with the rest of the Vera platform.
